### The rules

Ultimate is not a complicated game. You can't run with the disc. The field is 70 yards long, 40 yards wide, with two 20 yard end zones. Catching the disc in the endzone gets you one point. Games are played to a score, usually 13 or 15. One team is on offense, the other is on defense, and if the disc hits the ground or goes out of bounds, this swaps without a stoppage of play. Games are played to a score, usually 13 or 15.

## four (three?) steps of progression from learning the rules to learning the plays to breaking the plays
Learning frisbee has about three stages:
1. You learn the literal rules, as above. You are a complete beginner.
2. You learn the plays, tactics, etc.
3. You learn how to do these things _well_
4. You learn to break the rules.

## Mentality and mood is more than half the battle
When I was a captain in college, easily the most important thing I did was manage the team's emotions. I'm fine and good enough at the frisbee strategy, but I improved our gameplay significantly more by making sure everyone was eating, wearing sunscreen, hydrating, and staying upbeat. Frisbee is very momentum based and if you're pissed off you play worse. 

## The easiest way to boost morale is to make a sick play
If everyone is down and you're loosing, by far the simplest way to turn things around is to make a sick play. Make a diving block, make a shoestring score, get a layout D. The whole team will be hyped in an instant. It is also your responsibility, if you are on the sideline, to freak tf out on how cool the play was and what a badass your teammate is. That is your role to play in that play.


## There is no "should have won that game"
Frisbee is not huge, so you end up playing the same teams tournament over tournament and year over year. Everyone gets sifted into their respective rankings on the tier list and, for the most part, stay there. It's common thing to be driving to a tournament, chatting with your teammates about the last game you played against whomever your up against that day, or a past close game against a team that's a tier above you, and hear something along the lines of "Gah, we nearly had that, we should have one that game." You need to understand that "should have won that game" is truly devoid of meaning. If you should have won, you would have played better. There is no counterfactual in this life. Very rarely in sports do you actually get the benefit of a five or seven game series to find out who's best.
