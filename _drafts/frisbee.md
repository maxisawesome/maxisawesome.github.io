### Quickly, the rules

Little in the knowledge these lessons requires any understanding of how the actual act of playing frisbee works, but it's good context.

Ultimate is not a complicated game. You can't run with the disc. The field is 70 yards long, 40 yards wide, with two 20 yard end zones. There are seven players on each team. Catching the disc in the endzone gets you one point. Games are played to a score, usually 13 or 15. One team is on offense, the other is on defense, and if the disc hits the ground or goes out of bounds, this swaps without a stoppage of play. Games are played to a score, usually 13 or 15.

## It's easier to make a team better by adding someone new who is more skilled than it is to make someone who's already on the team better.

Getting better at anything is actually quite difficult. It is hard in frisbee bcus u dont have many reps, they are costly, and they are sample inefficient. There are many underdog stories of the team getting together, grinding it out, and overcoming the odds to get good enough to beat the big guy. These are impactful because having people improve considerably within the course of one season is difficult.

## Making opportunities vs easy opportuinities
There are two types of Ds you can get in frisbee (a D is when you are on defense and you knock the disc to the ground or catch it, putting your team on offensive and giving you a chance to score). The first are the type where you _should_ get the disc. It's thrown a little behind the person you're defending and you have an easy play. The disc is floating in the air, you get to the right position first, and you box out the person. Your man is running towards the disc and _slows down_ (a mistake), meaning you can keep running, move "through" them, and get to the disc before they do. Effectively, an opportunity is given to you and simple execution is the only thing needed to complete it. 

The other type of of D is one where you _create_ an opportunity. 



## Mentality and mood is more than half the battle
When I was a captain in college, easily the most important thing I did was manage the team's emotions. I'm fine and good enough at the frisbee strategy, but I improved our gameplay significantly more by making sure everyone was eating, wearing sunscreen, hydrating, and staying upbeat. Frisbee is very momentum based and if you're pissed off you play worse. 

## skill gives you defacto leadership 
look, if you're good, people will look up to you. It's irrelevant whether or not you have good leadership skills or good decision making abilities or competency in ppl management, your group will look to you for guidance. Use this if you so desire. I'd argue that if you are good becuase you want to do well, then you really want the team to do well, then this is an advantage you need to make use of. You see this everywhere - a good IC is more likely to be promoted to manager, even though the skillsets are completely seperate. If you are good, you should make the choice between embracing this or actively moving away from it.

## Leadership shines mostly when it's individually targetted.
I've captained two teams I've played on: my college team Detox and Riverside. You're effectiveness as a leader goes up significantly if you slightly tailor your style to each individual person. Some people need reencouragment after they make a mistake, some people need harsh words, some people need five minutes alone, but none of those are the same. Don't completely lose yourself, but tailor your style to the individuals you're leading.

## The easiest way to boost morale is to make a sick play
If everyone is down and you're loosing, by far the simplest way to turn things around is to make a sick play. Make a diving block, make a shoestring score, get a layout D. The whole team will be hyped in an instant. It is also your responsibility, if you are on the sideline, to freak tf out on how cool the play was and what a badass your teammate is. That is your role to play in that play.

## There is no "should have won that game"
Frisbee is not huge, so you end up playing the same teams tournament over tournament and year over year. Everyone gets sifted into their respective rankings on the tier list and, for the most part, stay there. It's common thing to be driving to a tournament, chatting with your teammates about the last game you played against whomever your up against that day, or a past close game against a team that's a tier above you, and hear something along the lines of "Gah, we nearly had that, we should have one that game." You need to understand that "should have won that game" is truly devoid of meaning. If you should have won, you would have played better. There is no counterfactual in this life. Very rarely in sports do you actually get the benefit of a five or seven game series to find out who's best. 


## There's plenty of glory in individual plays even if you're losing the battle.
You're not gonna win every game but execution to the highest order, giving a point your all, or a layout block when you're down by 5, has glory in and of itself. 

## Pressure is a privelege
Most people play (or work, or move through life) without much at stake. If the stakes are high and the pressure is on, it's because in that moment you're doing something worthwhile.
