Play Winning Chess by Yasser Seirawan

1. Castle early.  
2. More pieces in a certain area of the board will give you more power in that area.  
3. Don’t try to set a trap if it’s thwarting will lose you the game.  
4. 3 types of traps: at undefended pieces, at undefended pawns, at king’s bodyguards.  
   Gambit: sacrifice of a pawn (or other) for better development (more common for white)  
   Smothered Mate: King has very few squares to move as king and is “smothered” (usually don’t attack many squares, squares are just occupied).    
5. Never make a move banking on “Hope he doesn’t see this\!”  
6. King is strong in the endgame.  
7. King \+ Rook or Queen \-\> to checkmate, need king to be on an edge  
   1. Put king in a box then make it smaller  
   2. K+R: White (to win) wants their king close to the Black king. Black wants their king in the middle.   
   3. K+B or K+N cannot checkmate.

   Time: basically tempo, also similar to development, but kinda like how “really” you are in a certain area. Pushing the enemy queen around while you develop pieces is a great example of gaining time.

8. Attacking w/ many pieces is better than charging w/, say, just two.  
9. Don’t move a piece twice in the opening (you lose tempo in a critical time of the game  
   1. Developing a piece \> moving a pawn (generally)  
10. Advantages in time/development are temporary  
11. Rooks love open files\! (ones w/ no pawns)  
12. Forcing moves gains you time.  
    Space: an advantage in space means you control more territory than your opponent.   
13. Rooks need open files to be effective  
    1. When placing rooks \+ queens on open files, try to lead w/ your rooks  
    2. Placing rooks on the same file is very strong  
14. Bishops, like rooks, also prosper w/ open diagonals  
    Fianchettoed Bishop: a bishop on g2/b2 or g7/b7 \- great for “sniping” support  
15. Knights need to be advanced down the board to take advantage of their “short” movements. They are the strongest on the 6th rank.  
16. Space count is a big determining factor in positional advantages.  
17. Trading a piece that’s strong in space for one that’s weak in space is a bad trade.  
18. Destroy the defender the target will fall\!  
    1. Break into opponent’s pawn structures by attacking the weak pawns in them.  
19. In the opening, your moves should each do one of the following:  
    1. Acquire superior force in part of the board  
    2. Improve your pawn structure  
    3. Gain a lead in development  
    4. Gain Space  
    5. \*\* Don’t “entomb” your pieces (often, d3 is bad to move to w/ a minor piece)  
20. A player with less space should try to trade some pieces.   
21. Avoid trapping pieces w/ pawns\!

Pawn structure

* “Whoever has the most pawn islands loses”: groups of pawns sep by 1 file  
* Double pawns generally bad b/c no mobility (they’re alright in the center)  
  * Can be fine if you can defend the front one  
* Triple pawns are horrible\!  
* Isolated pawns are sad :(  
* How to (save? Line? Fine?) a weak pawns  
  * Avoid exposing them \-\> trailing pawn  
  * (Attackers should definitely attack them tho)  
  * (Before you attack a weak pawn, you must gain control of the square directly in front of it\!\!)  
  * Avoid backwards pawns  
* Passed pawns are good \- protected passed pawns are even better\!\!  
* Rooks on the 7th rank are very nice \- two rooks on the 7th rank are even better\!  
* Pawn chains are neither bad nor good  
  * Must attack them at their base, which is farther up than you might think sometimes.

Open Positions

* Few pawns in the center, lots of space to move pieces around  
* Castle quick\! And use rooks. Attacking will happen fast.  
* Bishops \> Knights w/o center pawns cause more room

Closed Positions

* Pawns in the center, a slower type of game  
* Attack wherever you have the most space to maneuver  
  * Getting open files for your rooks will be some hard work, but do it w/ pawn attacks (vs in open position, you attack mostly with your pieces)  
* Knights mostly \> Bishops bcus they can hope over stuff  
* Open positions are easier to play than closed positions

“Attack at every opportunity (but only after your pieces are developed) and be brutal in finishing off your opponent” → advice directed specifically to beginners. The point relates to open positions being easier than closed ones.
